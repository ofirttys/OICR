Project Overview

Welcome to our innovative implementation science project! You are joining a collaborative effort between The Hospital for Sick Children (SickKids) and Mount Sinai Fertility (MSF) to transform fertility preservation care for pediatric and adolescent cancer patients in Ontario.

Why This Matters: Fertility Preservation in Pediatric Cancer Care

Life-saving cancer treatments, including chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery, can significantly impact the future fertility of young cancer survivors. Many pediatric cancer patients are unaware of these irreversible effects until they reach adolescence or adulthood. Loss of fertility can cause significant emotional distress, depression, and impaired quality of life, affecting survivors' mental health, relationships, and future family-building aspirations.

What is Ovarian Tissue Cryopreservation (OTC)?

For children, adolescents, and young adults who cannot undergo ovarian stimulation, ovarian tissue cryopreservation (OTC) is the only viable method for preserving future fertility. This multi-step procedure involves:

  • Timely infertility risk assessment and oncofertility counselling
  • Surgical removal of an ovary (laparoscopic or abdominal surgery)
  • Transportation to an expert embryology lab for processing and cryopreservation
  • Long-term storage for potential future transplantation to restore fertility

The Challenge: Bridging the Gap

Despite being declared non-experimental in 2019 and recognized as best clinical practice, referral rates for OTC in Ontario remain strikingly low. Many eligible patients are not receiving timely fertility preservation counselling or referrals. Barriers exist at multiple levels including healthcare system constraints, organizational inefficiencies, provider knowledge gaps, and patient-related factors.

Our Solution: A Collaborative Fertility Preservation Program

SickKids and Mount Sinai Hospital have collaborated to develop Ontario's first formal fertility preservation program with a streamlined care pathway. This program is the first of its kind in Canada, integrating OTC into pediatric and AYA oncology care through infrastructure establishment, pathway development, and knowledge dissemination.

Implementation Science: The Study of Healthcare Practice Change

Implementation science is the study of methods to support the uptake of research findings and evidence-based practices into routine healthcare. Rather than asking "Does this intervention work?" (efficacy research), implementation science asks "How do we make this intervention work in real-world settings?" and "What factors help or hinder its adoption?"

Our Guiding Frameworks

This project uses four key implementation science frameworks:

  • RE-AIM Framework: Evaluating Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, and Maintenance
  • Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR): Understanding barriers and enablers
  • Proctor's Implementation Outcomes: Measuring acceptability, adoption, fidelity, and sustainability
  • Theoretical Framework of Acceptability (TfA): Assessing stakeholder perspectives

Getting Started - Suggested Learning Path

  1. Review this welcome package to understand the project scope and objectives
  2. Start with the videos - they provide excellent visual introductions to key concepts
  3. Read the core implementation science articles (especially the first three readings)
  4. Familiarize yourself with Canadian guidelines (CFAS guideline and position statement)
  5. Explore barrier literature to understand the challenges we're addressing
  6. Schedule your orientation meeting with the research team
